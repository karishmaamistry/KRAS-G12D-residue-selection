The Biological Engine: KRAS Dynamic Activation

Structural variations dictate target pocket geometry and drug-binding success.

01 · The Binary Switch

  • Dynamic Equilibrium: KRAS acts as a molecular switch, cycling between an active GTP-bound state and an inactive GDP-bound state.
  • The Switch Domains: High-mobility loop domains known as Switch I (residues 25–40) and Switch II (residues 60–76) dramatically rearrange their spatial conformation depending on the bound nucleotide.

02 · The G12D Oncogenic Target

  • The Mutation: The Gly12-to-Asp12 substitution introduces a bulky, negatively charged side chain directly into the nucleotide-binding pocket.
  • The Design Goal: Therapeutics aim to lock the protein into an inactive state by targeting pocket indentations immediately adjacent to the mutated site.

The Sequence & Geometry Trap

High confidence in a wild-type model cannot validate a mutant pocket.

01 · Sequence Mismatch

  • Direct Mismatch: Downloaded structure is canonical Wild-Type (Gly12, 189 aa); the assay construct is the G12D mutant (Asp12, 169 aa).
  • C-Terminal Tail: Downloaded model includes a 20-residue tail (170–189) absent in the assay construct.

02 · The Confidence Illusion

  • Method Artifact: The apparent regional confidence drop in the mutant fold is a prediction-method baseline effect, not a mutation penalty. A wild-type control fold yielded nearly identical low scores.
